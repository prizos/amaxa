8 MHz, 8 pF load, ESR 80 Ω maximum, ±10 ppm at 25 °C, ±20 ppm over temperature,
5.0 × 3.2 mm. Extended, stock 941, $0.27. Listing from JLCPCB's API on
2026-09-17; **shunt capacitance under 5 pF and drive level 10 µW typical,
200 µW maximum** from HCI's datasheet, served by LCSC.

## Chosen on gain margin, not stock

ST's AN2867 wants the MCU's maximum critical transconductance to be at least
five times what the crystal needs:

    gm_crit = 4 x ESR x (2 pi f)^2 x (C0 + CL)^2

The H743 offers 1.5 mA/V (datasheet Table 43), so the crystal may need at most
0.3 mA/V. At 8 MHz and 70–80 Ω that caps C0 + CL near 20 pF.

- The best-stocked 8 MHz Basic part, YXC X49SM8MSD2SC (C12674, HC-49S-SMD,
  124,702 in stock) has a **20 pF** load: it fails on CL alone, whatever its C0.
- Every in-stock 3225 part found had **250 Ω** ESR.
- This part: 80 Ω, 8 pF, C0 at most 5 pF — margin **11** at its worst.

The first crystal code reached for, C9002, turned out to be a **12 MHz** part.

## Load capacitors and stray

8.2 pF C0G each. Two in series make 4.1 pF, and the pin and board capacitance
alongside them makes up the rest. ST's datasheet suggests 10 pF as a rough
estimate of that stray, which would ask for *negative* capacitors with an 8 pF
crystal; the crystal sits a few millimetres from its pins, and the design states
3–5 pF instead. **This is the number to confirm at bring-up**, by measuring the
oscillator's frequency error.

**Footprint.** KiCad stock `Crystal:Crystal_SMD_5032-2Pin_5.0x3.2mm`, unmodified.
A two-terminal crystal: which pad is which does not matter.
