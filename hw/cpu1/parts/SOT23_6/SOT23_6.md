# SOT23_6 — the trip comparators and the USB protection

| | |
|---|---|
| Component | `COMPARATOR` `ESD_USB` |
| Manufacturer | Texas Instruments |
| Part number | `TLV3501AIDBVR`, `USBLC6-2SC6` |
| LCSC | [C193413](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C193413.html), [C7519](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C7519.html) |
| Footprint | `SOT-23-6` |

Single comparator, 4.5 ns, push-pull, 2.7 to 5.5 V. Stock 3,353, read from
JLCPCB's component API on 2026-09-17. Seven of them on the board.

## Seven singles, not two quads

The plan asked for two quad comparators. A quad this fast does not exist in
stock: the choice on the day was 4.5 ns in singles and duals, or the LM339's
300 ns in quads. **300 ns is six times the whole trip budget**, so the part
count went up instead.

Seven, because bipolar current sensing needs two trip points per phase - the
signal idles mid-scale and an over-current leaves that point in either
direction - plus one for the DC link.

The dual TLV3502 would have been four packages instead of seven, and KiCad has
no symbol for it: its pinout is unusual enough that no other part's symbol
fits. The single's symbol is `Comparator:TLV3501AIDBV`, and its six pins match
the datasheet's own SOT-23 column exactly.

## Figures used from the datasheet

TI's *TLV3501, TLV3502*, SBOS321E (March 2005, revised April 2016), served by
LCSC for C193413.

| Figure | Value | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Supply voltage | 2.7 to 5.5 V | §6.3 |
| Propagation delay | 4.5 ns typ, 7 ns max, 20 mV overdrive, −40 to +125 °C | §6.7 |
| | 7.5 ns typ, 5 mV overdrive at 25 °C | §6.7 |
| Input offset voltage | ±6.5 mV maximum | §6.6 |
| Input hysteresis | 6 mV | §6.6 |
| Common-mode range | (V−) − 0.2 V to (V+) − 0.2 V | §6.6 |
| Output swing from rail | 50 mV at ±1 mA | §6.6 |

**The overdrive matters more than the headline number.** 7 ns is at 20 mV past
the threshold; at 5 mV it is half as fast again. What decides which of those
this board gets is the slew of the signal at the tap, which is the M6 network's
problem, and the reason the tap is taken ahead of the anti-alias filter rather
than after it.

**6 mV of built-in hysteresis is why there are no external resistors around
these.** A comparator without it chatters on the way through its threshold, and
a chattering trip line is a latch that sets itself.

## Why the inputs go the way they do

Every one of the seven is wired so its **output falls when the trip condition
is true**, which is what lets seven push-pull outputs share one bus through
Schottky diodes. For a rising trip - over-current, over-voltage - the signal is
on the inverting input; for a falling one, on the non-inverting.

That also makes an unprogrammed board safe. The DAC **powers up** at zero —
its EEPROM ships that way, Table 4-2, cropped at `parts/MSOP10/evidence/` — so
every rising threshold is zero, every input sitting at its mid-scale idle is
already past it, and the board trips as it powers up.

**Power-up, not reset**, and this file used to say "comes out of reset at
zero", which is the stronger claim and the false one. The MCP4728 has no reset
pin, it sits on 3V3 which does not drop when `NRST` is asserted, and its only
other reset path is an I2C general-call that needs a working bus. After a
watchdog reset the thresholds are whatever the firmware that just stopped last
wrote. The latch is preset in that case too, so the board still comes up with
its outputs off — but by the latch, not by these thresholds. `cpu1.py` says
the same thing at `trip_block`, and the two now agree.

## Pin mapping

Pin 1 −IN, 2 V−, 3 +IN, 4 V+, 5 OUT, 6 SHDN, from the datasheet's Pin Functions
table. SHDN is tied low, which is the enabled state: a comparator idling in
shutdown is a trip point that does not exist.

**Footprint.** KiCad stock `Package_TO_SOT_SMD:SOT-23-6`, unmodified.

## The second part in this package: the USB ESD array

ST's `USBLC6-2SC6`, `C7519`, stock 36,759 on 2026-09-17. ST's own part rather
than one of the dozen clones LCSC lists under the same number: an ESD array is
bought entirely for the figures in its datasheet, and this is the one whose
datasheet could be read.

### Figures used from the datasheet

ST's *USBLC6-2*, Doc ID 11265 Rev 5 (October 2011).

| Figure | Value | Where |
|---|---|---|
| Peak pulse voltage, IEC 61000-4-2 contact | ±15 kV | Table 1 |
| Peak pulse voltage, IEC 61000-4-2 air | ±15 kV | Table 1 |
| Stand-off voltage V_RM | 5.25 V, 150 nA max leakage | Table 2 |
| Breakdown V_BR, VBUS to GND | 6 V min at 1 mA | Table 2 |
| Clamping V_CL | 12 V at 1 A, 17 V at 5 A, 8/20 µs | Table 2 |
| Capacitance, I/O to GND | 2.5 pF typ, 3.5 pF max | Table 2 |
| Capacitance, I/O to I/O | 1.2 pF typ, 1.7 pF max | Table 2 |

**The features page and the ratings table disagree, and the table is right.**
The features list quotes "level 4: 15 kV air, 8 kV contact", which are the
*standard's* level-4 thresholds. Table 1 is what the part survives: 15 kV
either way. `esd_contact_discharge` is the table's figure.

**15 kV is not 15 V.** The same trap as the RS-485 transceiver's ±18: an ESD
rating and a DC bus rating can be the same number and mean nothing like each
other.

### Pin mapping

1 I/O1, 2 GND, 3 I/O2, 4 I/O2, 5 VBUS, 6 I/O1 — from the functional diagram on
page 1, which is a top view of the SOT23-6L. Each channel appears twice, once
on each side, and the part is used with the cable on one side and the board on
the other.

**Which channel D+ takes is a layout decision, not a wiring one.** The two
channels are identical, so the only thing it changes is whether the pair keeps
to the same sides of itself from the package to the connector or crosses inside
the array — and a pair cannot cross without a via in the middle of a
controlled-impedance run. D+ takes I/O2 here for that reason, and the comment
in `cpu1.py` says so.

**VBUS sits on the pad between the pair's two.** The fan-out at that end is
deliberately not at the pair's spacing: at 0.452 mm apart the two tracks would
run one along each side of a pad they have nothing to do with. They step wide,
clear pad 5, and come together after it.
