# SOT23_6 — the trip comparators

| | |
|---|---|
| Component | `COMPARATOR` |
| Manufacturer | Texas Instruments |
| Part number | `TLV3501AIDBVR` |
| LCSC | [C193413](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C193413.html) |
| Footprint | `SOT-23-6` |

Single comparator, 4.5 ns, push-pull, 2.7 to 5.5 V. Stock 3,353, read from
JLCPCB's component API on 2026-09-17. Seven of them on the board.

## Seven singles, not two quads

The plan asked for two quad comparators. A quad this fast does not exist in
stock: the choice on the day was 4.5 ns in singles and duals, or the LM339's
300 ns in quads. **300 ns is six times the whole trip budget**, so the part
count went up instead.

Seven, because bipolar current sensing needs two trip points per phase - the
signal idles mid-scale and an over-current leaves that point in either
direction - plus one for the DC link.

The dual TLV3502 would have been four packages instead of seven, and KiCad has
no symbol for it: its pinout is unusual enough that no other part's symbol
fits. The single's symbol is `Comparator:TLV3501AIDBV`, and its six pins match
the datasheet's own SOT-23 column exactly.

**The overdrive matters more than the headline number.** 7 ns is at 20 mV past
the threshold; at 5 mV it is half as fast again. What decides which of those
this board gets is the slew of the signal at the tap, which is the M6 network's
problem, and the reason the tap is taken ahead of the anti-alias filter rather
than after it.

**6 mV of built-in hysteresis is why there are no external resistors around
these.** A comparator without it chatters on the way through its threshold, and
a chattering trip line is a latch that sets itself.

## Why the inputs go the way they do

Every one of the seven is wired so its **output falls when the trip condition
is true**, which is what lets seven push-pull outputs share one bus through
Schottky diodes. For a rising trip - over-current, over-voltage - the signal is
on the inverting input; for a falling one, on the non-inverting.

That also makes an unprogrammed board safe. The DAC comes out of reset at zero,
so every rising threshold is zero, so every phase current sitting at its
mid-scale idle is already past it and the board trips as it powers up.

## Pin mapping

Pin 1 −IN, 2 V−, 3 +IN, 4 V+, 5 OUT, 6 SHDN, from the datasheet's Pin Functions
table. SHDN is tied low, which is the enabled state: a comparator idling in
shutdown is a trip point that does not exist.
