300 mA LDO, 3.3 V fixed, 2 % accuracy, 68 dB PSRR at 1 kHz, 48 µVrms of its
own noise. Stock 44,531, extended library, read from JLCPCB's component API on
2026-09-22. One of them on the board.

## It is here for a voltage, not for noise

The analog header carries the supply the power board's sensors run from. It
was the 5 V rail through a 600 Ω ferrite, which is a perfectly good way to
keep switching noise off a sensor supply and the wrong answer to a question
nobody had asked.

Those sensors drive the sense lines, and the sense lines come back into TT_xx
analog inputs. ST's Table 20 caps those at **4.0 V absolute** — and Table 21
rates positive injection on them at minus five to *plus nought* milliamps, so
there is no positive-injection path to be inside of. The limit is a voltage
with nothing below it. An op-amp rails to its own supply when the thing it is
measuring goes wrong, which is precisely the over-current the trip chain
exists for, so a 5 V sensor supply is a 5.25 V fault waiting to happen.

`docs/research/07` answered that with "RC corner ~1–2 MHz **plus clamps**".
The RC is built. The clamps do not fit: a clamp has to sit on the sense net
ahead of the series resistor, and three positions were tried and built before
the requirement was written down and handed to a board that does not exist
yet.

Regulating the rail instead makes the fault impossible rather than survivable.
At the top corner of this part's own 2 % accuracy a sensor can rail to
**3.366 V**, which is inside the pin's rating with 0.6 V to spare.
`test_no_analog_input_may_be_presented_more_than_its_pin_allows` derives that
from the part's declared output and accuracy, so it moves if the part does.

**What it costs is at the connector.** A power board wanting 5 V sensors —
ACS724, a LEM module — makes 5 V **on its own side**, from the gate-drive
supply it has by definition, and scales their output to this rail. That is now
what the interface says, rather than "do not present me more than 4.0 V, ever".

This paragraph said "from the 12–15 V aux pin it already has on the digital
header" until the pins were counted. There is no aux pin: the digital header
carries 52 pins and 3V3 is the only supply on it. The mitigation survives — a
power board generating gate drive has a 12–15 V rail whether or not it crosses
the joint — but it was being described as something this connector provided,
and it is not.

The noise improvement is real and is the second reason: 68 dB of PSRR in front
of sensors feeding a 12-bit converter, where what was there before was buck
ripple through a bead.

## Figures used from the datasheet

TI's *TLV702xx 300-mA Low-IQ Low-Dropout Regulator*, SLVSAG6B (September 2010,
revised February 2011), served by LCSC for C26833. The whole electrical table
is committed at `evidence/tlv70233_electrical.png`.

| Figure | Value | Held against |
|---|---|---|
| Input voltage range | 2.0 to 5.5 V | the 4.75–5.25 V rail feeding it |
| DC output accuracy | ±2 %, −40 to +125 °C | `analog.supply_drop`, and the 4.0 V pin limit |
| Dropout | 375 mV max at 300 mA | the headroom from 4.75 V in to 3.366 V out |
| Output current | 300 mA | `analog.supply_current`, at this board's 50 % derating |
| Ground pin current | 55 µA max at no load | the 5 V rail's own budget |
| Output capacitance | 0.1 µF *effective* minimum | the 1 µF + 100 nF fitted |
| Input capacitance | 0.1 to 1.0 µF when the source is distant | the 1 µF fitted |

**"Effective" is TI's own word**, and it means after bias and temperature
derating. That is the question this board records as unresolved for its buck
converters' capacitors — but here the nominal fitted is an order of magnitude
above the requirement, so no plausible derating reaches it.

The 200 mΩ ESR ceiling that goes with the output figure is **not declared as a
parameter**, because no capacitor on this board states an ESR and it would be
a float with nothing to compare against. A 0402 X5R is milliohms; the ceiling
exists for tantalums and this is not one.

## Enable is tied to the input

The part has an EN pin and this board does not use it: the analog rail lives
and dies with 5 V, and there is no sequencing requirement — VREF+ comes from
3V3 and the sensors are on the other side of a connector. V_EN(HI) is 0.9 V
minimum against V_IN, so tying the two together is the datasheet's own
arrangement for an always-on rail.

It is tied at the **track**, not across the package: IN and EN sit either side
of the ground pad on a SOT-23-5, so a link between them would have to cross
it. Both come off the same 5 V column instead, which the layout comment says.

## The symbol and the footprint

Symbol `Regulator_Linear:TLV70233_SOT23-5`, which KiCad ships for this exact
part number. Footprint is KiCad stock `Package_TO_SOT_SMD:SOT-23-5`,
unmodified: pads 1.325 × 0.6 mm at ±1.1375 mm, which is what makes the pad
reach 1.8 mm from the centreline and is why the 5 V column could not be run
past its side.
