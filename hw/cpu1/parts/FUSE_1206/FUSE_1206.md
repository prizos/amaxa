1.5 A slow-blow, 63 V DC, 50 A interrupting rating, 1206. Stock 2,120, read
from LCSC on 2026-09-19.

The same part led12 uses, and for the same reason: the **468** series is
slow-blow, while the 466 that was first drawn there is fast-acting despite the
name suggesting otherwise. See `hw/led12/parts/FUSE_1206/FUSE_1206.md` for that
story.

## Why 1.5 A, and why it used to say 1 A

At the lowest input, 9 V, the board draws **0.68 A** at the terminal.
`test_the_fuse_carries_the_board_and_opens_at_the_rail_it_protects` derives
that from the rails' declared budgets and the efficiency the design assumes,
and asks for 1.5× on top, because a fuse ages every time it runs warm. 0.68 ×
1.5 = 1.02 A, so a 1 A part misses.

**It used to read "about 0.5 A", and that was the defect.** The 3V3 buck runs
from the 5 V rail, so everything the logic rail delivers is drawn again
through the 5 V one — but the two budgets were only ever added on the copper,
never in the arithmetic. `_reflected_current` in `checks/test_power.py` adds
them now, and the same correction is what re-sized this fuse: the old figure
left out three quarters of the board.

The FET behind the fuse carries 2.3 A, so it still passes more than this fuse
will — `test_the_fet_carries_what_the_fuse_allows` holds that ordering, and it
is why 2 A was not chosen instead.

**63 V matters more than the 1 A here.** The fuse is what a sustained
overvoltage has to open, and between the TVS's 40 V stand-off and the top of
its breakdown band the TVS does nothing — so the rail can sit at 51.1 V with
the fuse the only thing left. The check compares the two.

The interrupting rating is not checked against anything: the fault current
available at the terminal is a property of whatever is wired to it, not of this
board. `checks/config.py` records that.

**Footprint.** KiCad stock `Fuse_1206_3216Metric`, unmodified.
