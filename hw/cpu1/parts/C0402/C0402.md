All read from JLCPCB's component API on 2026-09-17, except the Ethernet
crystal load, read on 2026-09-20 when it changed.

**Why C0G for the crystals.** Load capacitors set the oscillator's frequency;
X5R and X7R move with temperature and bias, C0G does not.

**Why the Ethernet load is 36 pF and ±1 %, where it used to be 33 pF and ±5 %.**
The crystal is cut for 20 pF, and what it sees is its two capacitors in series
with the PHY's 3 pF pins and the board's own copper alongside them. The copper
is now measured off the board file rather than declared - 2.08 pF on one leg
and 1.22 on the other, because a four-pad crystal puts its terminals on a
diagonal and one track goes the long way round. With 33 pF the crystal saw
18.8 pF against a 20 pF cut; 36 pF sees 20.3, and it is the nearest value the
E24 series offers. `test_the_crystal_sees_the_load_it_is_cut_for`
picks that value itself and fails naming it.

The tolerance is the second half of it. This crystal is ±20 ppm cut and ±20 ppm
over temperature, and ages ±5 ppm a year, against the 50 ppm IEEE 802.3 gives
the whole link - so there is almost nothing left to spend. At ±5 % the
capacitors alone move the load ±0.9 pF; at ±1 % they move it ±0.18. The part
costs the same and is the one place on this board where a tight capacitor buys
something measurable. Its stock is 7,340 rather than the million the ±5 % part
had, which is ample for this board and worth watching if it is ever built in
quantity.

**VCAP's ESR.** ST asks for under 100 mΩ (datasheet Table 24). A 2.2 µF 0402
MLCC is well under that at the regulator's frequencies, but the listing does not
state it, so no check does either.

**C0G for the 220 pF**, and the datasheet asks for it by name: this capacitor
couples the ripple ramp into the feedback node, and an X7R's capacitance falls
with bias in a place where the value is the thing being relied on.

**Why 220 pF and not TI's 56 pF.** Equation 26 sizes it from the settling time
wanted and the feedback divider's top resistor, and this board's divider is
158 k where TI's reference design uses 446 k. Carrying their capacitor across
without their resistor satisfied the equation only to 26.5 µs instead of their
75. 220 pF gives 98 µs at the tolerance corner, where 150 pF gives 67 and
misses. See `../SO8EP/SO8EP.md`; stock read 2026-09-19.

**The 6.3 V part** sees the core regulator's output, about 1.2 V. Every other
capacitor sits on the 3.3 V rail or below; a check holds all of them above the
rail's highest voltage.

**Footprint.** KiCad stock `Capacitor_SMD:C_0402_1005Metric`, unmodified.
