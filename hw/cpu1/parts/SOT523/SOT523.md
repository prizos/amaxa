Read from JLCPCB's component API on 2026-09-22. SOT-523 is 1.6 × 1.6 mm — a
third the area of the SOT-23 this nearly became, which matters because it goes
into the buffer fan-out lane rather than beside it.

## Why this part and not a bigger one

The channel is almost irrelevant here: 150 Ω sits in series with the drain to
bound what the FET takes from a buffer output that has not let go yet, so
on-resistance of half an ohm or five makes no difference to the fall time.

What matters is the **gate**, because it hangs on `TRIPPED` — the same net that
disables both '541s. Every picofarad there delays the whole trip chain, not
just this transistor. C_iss is 60.67 pF, which a latch output charges to the
threshold in 2.5 ns. An AO3400A, which was the first candidate because it is a
JLCPCB Basic part with on-resistance quoted at 2.5 V, has **630 pF** — 38 ns to
turn on, most of the budget, for a channel this circuit does not need.

## The figures are specified where the part is used

This is the reason for this part over the obvious ones. `V_GS` here is the 3V3
rail at its low corner, 3.135 V. A 2N7002 quotes on-resistance at 10 V and a
BSS138 at 4.5 or 10 V, so neither has a number that applies — using them would
mean asserting a fall time from a figure measured at three times the drive.

Diodes' DS30599 gives R_DS(on) at **4.5 V, 2.5 V and 1.8 V**. The 2.5 V row is
a maximum at a drive below this board's minimum, so it is conservative by
construction.

The drain carries 16 mA during the buffer's 7 ns of disable and nothing
afterwards, so the current and voltage ratings are not close to anything.

## Pin mapping

SOT-523: pin 1 gate, pin 2 source, pin 3 drain, which is what
`Transistor_FET:Q_NMOS_GSD` numbers. Same arrangement as the SOT-23 small-signal
convention the package descends from.
