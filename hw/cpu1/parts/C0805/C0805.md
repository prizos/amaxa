The 10 µF is the same part led12 uses. Two of them make the 100 V buck's output
capacitance and one more the 3V3 converter's input, which its datasheet puts at
10 µF or more.

The 22 µF pair is the 3V3 rail's own output capacitance. Its value is not free:
the converter has no external compensation, and
`test_the_3v3_output_filter_is_the_one_the_datasheet_specifies` holds the whole
rail — these two and every decoupling capacitor on 3V3 — inside the 20 to 68 µF
the datasheet's Table 2 gives for a 3.3 V output.

## 25 V, for a 3.3 V rail

The 22 µF used to be a 10 V X7R, and the rating was not about standing off the
rail — 10 V clears 3.3 V comfortably. It is about `capacitors.bias_derating`:
a Class II ceramic loses capacitance in proportion to the field across it, and
this board assumes it loses as large a fraction of its value as the fraction
of its rating it is operated at. At 10 V rated and 3.465 V of bias that is a
third gone; at 25 V it is an eighth.

That turns the 20–68 µF window from something the pair only met on the label
into something they meet at the bias they actually sit at: 30.3 µF from the
two of them at the tolerance corner, against 20 µF wanted, with the rail's
decoupling on top. The ceiling is cleared by the label rather than the derated
value, because a ceramic does not gain capacitance.

X5R rather than the X7R it was: the part with the rating that matters is
Samsung's basic-library one, and four and a half million in stock against
thirteen thousand is worth more here than the difference between two Class II
temperature grades on a rail whose window has 30 µF of room in it.
