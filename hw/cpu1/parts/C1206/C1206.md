22 µF ±10 %, 25 V, X5R. Stock 879,433, basic library, read from JLCPCB's
component API on 2026-09-22. One of them on the board.

## The only capacitor here with nothing beside it

The TPS562200's datasheet asks for 10 µF or more at its input pin, and
`test_each_converter_has_its_bootstrap_and_input_capacitance` reads that
requirement *at the pin* rather than on the rail — deliberately, because
without the restriction the 5 V buck's own output bulk and seven comparator
bypasses counted towards it and the part actually fitted could have been
deleted with the check still passing.

So this one part has to meet the whole 10 µF on its own, which is what makes
the two things taken off it matter:

| | |
|---|---|
| tolerance | ±10 %, so 19.8 µF at the corner |
| DC bias | 5.25 V on a 25 V part, a fifth of rating, so ×0.79 |
| what the pin sees | **15.6 µF**, against 10 µF asked for |

It was a 22 µF ±20 % rated **10 V**, sitting at 5.25 V — half its rating.
Under `capacitors.bias_derating` that is half its capacitance, and 17.6 µF of
tolerance corner becomes 8.8 µF against a 10 µF minimum. The part failed the
requirement and the check did not notice, because it compared the label.

## Why the rating rather than more parts

Bias loss is proportional to the field. Doubling the capacitors keeps the
field the same and buys nothing but area; raising the rating lowers the field
and buys the capacitance back. That is also why this is the only change here
that needed a bigger *case*: 25 V at 22 µF does not fit an 0805 in the basic
library, and ±10 % rather than ±20 % is worth having on the one capacitor with
no neighbour to share its shortfall.

**Footprint.** KiCad stock `Capacitor_SMD:C_1206_3216Metric`, unmodified.
