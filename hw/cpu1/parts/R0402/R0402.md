## The 22 ohm

Series into each of the eight fast analog channels, with 4.7 nF to ground at
the pin. Far below the hundreds of ohms that filter would normally use,
because the resistor has a second job: putting back the charge the converter's
own 4 pF sampling capacitor takes at each sample, inside a 236 ns window.

It was 10 Ω and 10 nF, which satisfied that and the 1–2 MHz corner and was
still wrong. A comparator watches the same node, and the capacitor is a shunt
branch *on* that node: with the 2 Ω `header.source_impedance` in front of it
the tap is a lead-lag network, arriving 17 % low on a step against 12 % of
declared threshold tolerance, and delaying a ramp by Rs·C = 20 ns — the entire
share of the trip budget that had been reserved for "a filter this board has
not drawn", which turned out to have been fitted all along.

Three constraints, and 22 Ω with 4.7 nF is where they meet: 1.27–1.73 MHz
corner, 0.37 LSB settled of the half-LSB allowed, 8.3 % step error and 10.6 ns
of ramp delay. `checks/test_adc.py` computes all four, and
`test_a_trip_stops_the_outputs_inside_the_budget` now spends the delay out of
the budget instead of reserving for it.

## The 33 ohm

One in series with each of the fifteen buffered outputs, between the buffer and
the connector. It damps what a ribbon cable to a gate driver reflects, and at
33 Ω into a CMOS input it drops nothing a gate driver would notice. The value
is bounded at both ends by
`checks/test_safety.py::test_every_buffered_output_has_a_series_resistor_and_a_pull_down`:
much less and it damps nothing, much more and it slows the edge it is
protecting.

## The 150 ohm, and why the pull-downs stayed at 10 k

One resistor, between Q2's drain and GATE_ENABLE_OUT. The pull-downs were
briefly 1.2 kΩ for a reason that did not survive arithmetic, and the trail is
worth keeping because the trap is easy to walk back into.

The static job of a gate-line pull-down is the one they were sized for — an
unfitted power board is not a command, and any value that beats leakage does
that. The second job was never costed: when a trip puts the buffers into high
impedance, the pull-down is the only thing discharging the gate line, and
`trip.budget`'s own words are "how long a half bridge survives a
shoot-through", which is not the instant the buffer lets go. At 10 kΩ, with
the copper measured off the routed board and 10 pF allowed for the far side,
the worst line took **205 ns** to reach a valid low, against a 50 ns budget.

**There is no resistor that fixes it.** The budget wants 600 Ω or less.
Fifteen of those across two '541s, at 3.465 V, is more than the packages may
pass — `test_the_buffers_drive_less_than_they_are_rated_for` walks that out —
and the same fifteen are a continuous draw on the 3V3 rail, which wants
1.4 kΩ or more before the rail's own budget moves. The window between the two
is empty, and 1.2 kΩ sat in it: it met neither, and it pushed the 3V3 rail to
806 mA against its 800 mA.

So the fall is done by a transistor on the one line that means *stop*. Q2
shorts GATE_ENABLE_OUT to ground through this 150 Ω as soon as the latch
trips, and the line falls in 3 ns instead of 205. The value is bounded at both
ends by `checks/test_safety.py`:

| | |
|---|---|
| Below ~111 Ω | the buffer still driving that line is asked for more than the 24 mA it wants to stay inside |
| Above ~1 kΩ | the fall stops fitting the budget with the rest of the chain in it |

150 Ω takes 19 mA from the buffer through the 33 Ω already in series, and
passes 23 mA at the first instant against the DMG1012T's 800 mA. Both ends are
computed by the checks, not by this table. The fourteen PWM lines keep their 10 kΩ and
their 205 ns, which the pin map now states as what it is: a second layer
behind the gate kill, not the thing the timing rests on.

## What the 1 % is doing

On the pull-downs, nothing — any tolerance would do. On the five resistors that
set a rail or a threshold it is the whole point: every one of those checks works
the declared tolerance through both ends, and
`test_the_3v3_feedback_divider_holds_the_rail_inside_its_band` has under 3 % of
headroom at the top with 1 % parts. A 5 % series would not fit.

**The 226 kΩ has the thinnest stock on the board** at 4,400 pieces. It sets the
input lockout with the 49.9 kΩ below it, so a substitute has to keep the ratio
and be checked against `input.lockout` rather than swapped by value.

## The bus terminations left this package

Both used to be 0402s. Neither is now: the RS-485 termination is a 1206 and
CAN's two split halves are 0805s, in `parts/R1206` and `parts/R0805`, and the
reason is that an 0402 could not take its own transceiver's output. 103 mW
into a 62.5 mW part is past the absolute maximum, not merely past this board's
50 % derating, and 58 V of declared CAN fault across a part rated 50 V is a
second limit that has nothing to do with power. See those notes.
