## The 10 ohm

Series into each of the nine fast analog channels, with 10 nF to ground at the
pin. Ten ohms rather than the hundred that filter would normally use, because
the resistor has a second job: putting back the charge the converter's own 4 pF
sampling capacitor takes at each sample, inside a 236 ns window.
`checks/test_adc.py` works both constraints at once, and there is no value that
satisfies one without the other being computed.

## The 33 ohm

One in series with each of the fifteen buffered outputs, between the buffer and
the connector. It damps what a ribbon cable to a gate driver reflects, and at
33 Ω into a CMOS input it drops nothing a gate driver would notice. The value
is bounded at both ends by
`checks/test_safety.py::test_every_buffered_output_has_a_series_resistor_and_a_pull_down`:
much less and it damps nothing, much more and it slows the edge it is
protecting.

## What the 1 % is doing

On the pull-downs, nothing — any tolerance would do. On the five resistors that
set a rail or a threshold it is the whole point: every one of those checks works
the declared tolerance through both ends, and
`test_the_3v3_feedback_divider_holds_the_rail_inside_its_band` has under 3 % of
headroom at the top with 1 % parts. A 5 % series would not fit.

**The 226 kΩ has the thinnest stock on the board** at 4,400 pieces. It sets the
input lockout with the 49.9 kΩ below it, so a substitute has to keep the ratio
and be checked against `input.lockout` rather than swapped by value.

## The bus terminations are sacrificial once their jumper is closed

Both field buses' terminations sit behind a `SolderJumper_2_Open`, so on a
board as fabricated there is no DC path across either bus: CAN's lower
resistor meets a 4.7 nF capacitor on its far side and the RS-485 one stops at
the open jumper. Nothing dissipates, and
`test_a_bus_termination_survives_the_fault_its_transceiver_declares` walks the
copper to establish that rather than taking anyone's word for it.

**Close either jumper and the arithmetic changes.** Each transceiver declares
the voltage its bus may be driven to under a fault — 58 V for the CAN part,
18 V for the RS-485 one — and across the termination that is:

| | across | current | per resistor |
|---|---|---|---|
| CAN, 2 × 60.4 Ω split | 58 V | 0.48 A | **13.9 W** |
| RS-485, 120 Ω | 18 V | 0.15 A | **2.7 W** |

Against 62.5 mW. No 0402 survives either, and no resistor of any size survives
the CAN figure — a terminated node does not come through a bus short to
battery with its termination intact. That is normal for CAN, and it is why
termination belongs at the cable ends rather than at every node. The
transceiver itself is rated for the fault; the resistor is not, and closing
the jumper is the decision that makes it sacrificial.

The check fires the moment either jumper becomes a bridged one, which is how
this was measured.
