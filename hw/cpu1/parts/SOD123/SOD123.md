15 V Zener, 13.8 to 15.6 V, 500 mW. Stock 63,824, read from JLCPCB's component
API on 2026-09-17. The same part led12 uses.

## What it does here, and how that differs from led12

The reverse-polarity FET's gate sits at ground through 100 kΩ, so V_GS is the
whole input voltage — 36 V in normal operation, and up to the TVS's 64.5 V
clamp during a surge. Both are past the FET's ±20 V rating. This clamps it.

On led12 this Zener never conducts: that board's rail tops out at 13.2 V.
**Here it conducts whenever the input is above about 14 V, which is most of the
declared range, and that is the intent rather than a defect.** The current is
(V_in − V_z) / 100 kΩ — a fifth of a milliamp at 36 V — and
`test_the_gate_clamp_survives_conducting_through_a_surge` bounds the
dissipation in both the Zener and the resistor at the clamp voltage, which is
the worst case.

15 V is the middle of the only window there is: above it the FET's rating is
exceeded, below about 4 V the FET is not fully enhanced at the lowest input.
`test_the_gate_clamp_keeps_the_fet_inside_its_rating_and_still_turns_it_on`
holds both ends.

## Pin mapping

Pad 1 cathode, pad 2 anode, and the orientation is what makes it a clamp: the
cathode goes to the **source** and the anode to the gate, so it conducts when
the source is more than its voltage above the gate.

**Needs a human eye** against the manufacturer's drawing before fabrication —
the same caution as led12's note records for the same package.
