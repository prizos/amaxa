60.4 Ω ±1 %, 125 mW, 150 V, thick film. Stock 21,742, extended library, read
from JLCPCB's component API on 2026-09-22. Two of them on the board, the two
halves of one split termination.

## Why they are not 0402s

Closed onto the bus, the TCAN1044V drives up to 3.3 V differential into
120.8 Ω. Each half carries 27 mA and dissipates **46 mW**: inside an 0402's
62.5 mW absolute rating and past the 31 mW this board derates it to. The
0805's 125 mW puts it at 74 % of the derated limit.

The other limit is the one power does not cover. The TCAN1044V declares a
**58 V** bus fault voltage — this board already used that figure to size the
connector's contacts — and an 0402 is rated 50 V. A part can be inside its
power rating and still be flashed over, and nothing was asking. The 0805 is
rated 150 V.

Neither number survives the fault itself: 58 V across 120.8 Ω is 0.48 A and
13.9 W in each half. No chip resistor of any size takes that, which is why
termination belongs at the cable ends rather than at every node, and why both
jumpers ship open.

## Not a 1206, which is what the other termination became

Two of these stand in a chain between the bus lanes with a solder jumper above
them, in a window the transceiver's own leg and the connector's courtyard
close at 9 mm. Two 1206s need 4.56 mm between centres before clearance; two
0805s need 3.36. At 46 mW against 62.5 derated the 0805 does the job, and the
1206 would have cost the chain the room it does not have.

**Basic versus extended.** This is an extended-library part and the 1206 next
door is a basic one. There is no basic 0805 at 60.4 Ω in JLCPCB's catalogue —
the API returns none — so the choice was an extended 0805 or a basic part at a
value that is not what a split CAN termination is. 60.4 Ω is half of 120.8,
and the pair has to look like the cable.

**Footprint.** KiCad stock `Resistor_SMD:R_0805_2012Metric`, unmodified.
