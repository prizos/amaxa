## Why a 5032 and not the cheaper 3225

The PHY's oscillator is specified for crystals up to **30 Ω** of equivalent
series resistance. At 25 MHz a 3225 package runs 50 to 80 Ω — every in-stock
3225 found was 50 Ω or worse — and a crystal above the specification will
often still start on a warm bench. That is the worst available outcome: it
fails in the cold, in the field, intermittently.

The 5032 is a millimetre and a half wider and buys the margin.

## Two constraints that nearly had no common answer

This part had to satisfy both at once:

- **ESR ≤ 30 Ω**, from the PHY.
- **tolerance + stability ≤ ±45 ppm**, because IEEE 802.3 gives the whole link
  ±50 ppm and the datasheet's Note 5.15 reserves the rest for ageing.

Most of the shortlist met one and failed the other. `X322525MSB4SI` is ±10 and
±20 ppm — comfortable — and 50 Ω. `TAXM25M2QLFCDT1T` is 30 Ω and ±20/±30, which
sums to the whole budget with nothing left for ageing. This part is 30 Ω and
±20/±20, so it comes to ±40 ppm and clears both. `test_ethernet.py` holds it to
both, separately, so a substitution has to clear both too.

## Load capacitors

Cut for 20 pF. The two 33 pF capacitors either side, in series, plus the PHY's
3 pF per pin and the board's stray, come to the load the crystal expects — the
check works it out rather than taking 33 pF on trust, because a crystal
presented the wrong load runs at the wrong frequency, and the frequency is the
one thing the whole link is derived from.

## Needs a human eye

**The figures above come from LCSC's parametric data, not from a datasheet.**
Yajingxin's own document could not be fetched from any mirror reachable here.
For a resistor that would not matter; for the part the entire Ethernet clock is
derived from, ESR and the ppm figures are the whole reason this part was chosen
over two cheaper ones, and they should be confirmed against the manufacturer's
drawing before ordering.
