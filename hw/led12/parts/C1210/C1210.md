# C1210 — 1210 bulk capacitor

47 µF, 25 V, X5R, ±20 %, 1210. Stock 129,263, $0.2185 at qty 100. Extended.

**Why this part, and what changed.** The board first asked for 100 µF at 25 V
and named Samsung CL32A107MQVNNNE for it. That part is **6.3 V**, not 25 V — the
`Q` in the part number is the 6.3 V voltage code — so it would have failed the
moment 12 V was applied. Checking further, **100 µF at 25 V does not exist in a
1210 MLCC**: the only catalogue entry is a Samsung part with zero stock and no
product page. The largest genuinely purchasable 1210 at 25 V is 47 µF.

Alternatives considered and rejected:

- Taiyo Yuden TMK325ABJ476MM-P (C90142), 47 µF 25 V — LCSC stock zero.
- Tantalum. EIA case B is 3528 metric, which is **larger** than 1210 (3225), so
  no tantalum drops into this land pattern. Case A fits a 1206 and tops out at
  4.7 µF / 25 V.
- SMD aluminium can, e.g. 100 µF 25 V (C2836443) — cheaper and larger, but a
  6.3 × 7.7 mm cylinder, not a 1210 footprint.

**Derating, not yet modelled.** X5R at 25 V loses a large fraction of its
capacitance under 12 V DC bias. The declared 47 µF is the nominal value, which
is what gets ordered; the effective value belongs in the simulation decks. Until
those exist, treat the real bulk capacitance as materially lower than 47 µF.

**Pin mapping.** Symmetrical two-terminal part.

**Footprint.** KiCad stock `Capacitor_SMD:C_1210_3225Metric`, unmodified.
