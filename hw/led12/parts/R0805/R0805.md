## Why the LED series resistor is 2.2 k

The board first used 680 Ω, carried over from a 5 V way of thinking. On a 12 V
rail the LED drops about 2.2 V and the resistor takes the other 9.8 V, so at the
resulting 13.8 mA it dissipates **130 mW in a 125 mW part** — 104 % of its
rating at nominal, and 137 % at the worst corner. It would have cooked.

2.2 kΩ gives 3.7 to 5.1 mA across the supply range, at 45 % of the part's
rating, which satisfies the 50 % derating rule the checks enforce. At about
4 mA the chosen LED puts out roughly 12 mcd — an obvious indicator indoors.

Keeping 10 mA would have meant a 1206 resistor (250 mW) and a second package on
the board. Not worth it for an indicator.

**Trade-off recorded.** All four are JLCPCB *Extended* parts, which costs extra
on assembly setup. The equivalent UNI-ROYAL set is entirely *Basic* and cheaper
(0805W8F1002T5E / C17414, 0805W8F1003T5E / C149504, 0805W8F3301T5E / C26010 and
the 2.2 k equivalent), at the price of four supplier lines instead of one.
Worth revisiting if this board is ever assembled in quantity.
