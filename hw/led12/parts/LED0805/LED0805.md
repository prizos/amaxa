# LED0805 — 0805 red indicator LED

| | |
|---|---|
| Component | `LED_RED` |
| Manufacturer | Everlight |
| Part number | `17-21SURC/S530-A3/4T` |
| LCSC | [C2943978](https://www.lcsc.com/product-detail/C2943978.html) |
| Footprint | `LED_0805_2012Metric` |

Red, 624–632 nm, 60 mcd, **Vf 2.0–2.4 V at 20 mA**. Stock 196,650, $0.0147 at
qty 100. Extended.

**Why this part.** Chosen for the *bounded* forward voltage. The obvious cheap
alternative, NationStar NCD0805R1 (C84256, Basic, $0.0135), is specified
1.6–2.6 V at 25 mA — a 1 V spread on a 12 V rail through 680 Ω swings the branch
current by about 1.5 mA, and more importantly that figure is LCSC parametric
data that could not be confirmed against the datasheet, whose tables are images.
A 0.4 V spread that is written down in the datasheet is worth $0.0012 a part.

**Pin mapping.** Pad 1 cathode, pad 2 anode, per the KiCad footprint's own
convention. **Needs a human eye**, and for a reason worth writing down: **the
datasheet LCSC serves for C2943978 is another manufacturer's document.** LCSC's
own product page gives the part as EVERLIGHT `17-21SURC/S530-A3/4T`, but the
PDF behind it is a NATIONSTAR specification, so it cannot settle this part's
polarity. Everlight's own document is not reachable from here: their published
path for it returns 404 and their site search returns nothing.

What does hold in the meantime is the same argument as every other two-terminal
part on these boards — a chip LED is placed by its polarity mark against the
board's, not by a pad number, and
`test_every_polarised_part_marks_its_cathode_on_the_silkscreen` asserts that
the silkscreen bar is at the end the netlist calls the cathode. That makes a
backwards LED an assembly error rather than a design one. Confirm against
Everlight's drawing before release anyway.

**Footprint.** KiCad stock `LED_SMD:LED_0805_2012Metric`, unmodified.
